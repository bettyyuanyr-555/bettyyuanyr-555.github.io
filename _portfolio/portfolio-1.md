This project develops a statistical framework for studying how scientific discoveries emerge, concentrate, and diffuse across institutional and geographic knowledge networks. It uses large-scale bibliometric data to connect statistical inference with network structure and the science of science.

I built OpenAlex-based discovery corpora with entity disambiguation and non-research filtering. Kleinberg burst detection aligned with known takeoff periods in 79% of audited cases within ±5 years; citation-neighborhood expansion with semantic and citation ranking covered 84% of validated canonical papers in the top ten. A pilot audit achieved 86% topical precision, and preliminary citing-city ranks followed a heavy-tailed pattern (R² = 0.88). The analysis combines network statistics, statistical learning, and AI-assisted information extraction to characterize how ideas travel across research communities; it does not yet claim causal mechanisms.

**Status:** Working paper in progress.
