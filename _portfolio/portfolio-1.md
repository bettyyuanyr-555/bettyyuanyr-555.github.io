This project develops a statistical framework for studying how scientific discoveries emerge, concentrate, and diffuse across institutional and geographic knowledge networks. It uses large-scale bibliometric data to connect statistical inference with network structure and the science of science.

I built OpenAlex-based discovery corpora with entity disambiguation and non-research filtering, then combined Kleinberg burst detection with citation-neighborhood expansion, semantic ranking, and citation signals. Pilot audits showed strong topical precision, reliable identification of discovery takeoff periods, and broad coverage of canonical papers. Across 1900–2019, discoveries became increasingly dispersed across cities and countries while concentration in the leading cities declined; historical centres nevertheless retained a cumulative advantage in discovery output, knowledge continuity, and citation impact. The analysis combines network statistics, statistical learning, and AI-assisted information extraction to study how ideas travel across research communities.
