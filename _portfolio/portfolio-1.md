This project develops a statistical framework for studying how scientific knowledge emerges, concentrates, and diffuses across institutional and geographic networks. It uses large-scale bibliometric data to connect statistical inference with network structure and the science of science.

I built OpenAlex-based knowledge corpora with entity disambiguation and non-research filtering, then combined Kleinberg burst detection with citation-neighborhood expansion, semantic ranking, and citation signals. Pilot audits showed strong topical precision, reliable identification of knowledge takeoff periods, and broad coverage of canonical papers. Across 1900–2019, knowledge production became increasingly dispersed across cities and countries while concentration in the leading cities declined; historical centres nevertheless retained a cumulative advantage in output, knowledge continuity, and citation impact. The analysis combines network statistics, statistical learning, and AI-assisted information extraction to study how ideas travel across research communities.
