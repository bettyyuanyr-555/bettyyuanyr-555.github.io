As a remote research assistant at the University of Pennsylvania, I contributed to a systematic review of 28 HIV studies and an evidence map spanning HIV and Alzheimer's disease literatures. The project treats literature retrieval as a statistical measurement problem: how can AI systems identify relevant evidence while making uncertainty, ranking quality, and provenance transparent?

I built a PubMed retrieval pipeline comparing dense, BM25, and hybrid retrieval, and evaluated it with Recall@k, MRR, MAP@k, and NDCG. By combining information retrieval, statistical evaluation, and evidence tracing, the project aims to make AI-assisted literature synthesis more reproducible, auditable, and interpretable.

**Recognition:** Conference posters at AAIC Neuro Next 2026 and the Penn CFAR Scientific Symposium 2026.
